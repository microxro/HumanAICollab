StudyHold: Key Differentiators

1. Student-owned, not institution-gated

	•	Blackboard’s Scholar (scholar.com) is sold to schools/districts; the school is the customer, and student data sits under a “school official” FERPA relationship.
	•	StudyHold needs no district contract, IT procurement, or school adoption — a student can start using it in under a minute, entirely on their own.
	•	Data lives on-device by default; cloud sync and the parent portal are opt-in, not the foundation.

2. One connected record, not four disconnected apps

	•	Competitors are mostly single-purpose: a distraction blocker, a flashcard app, a college-app tracker, a study-room timer.
	•	A real student week cuts across all of those — StudyHold links them: a class’s grading policy feeds live GPA, a missed assignment can generate a calendar block, study sessions and flashcard decks tie back to the class they’re for.
	•	Value comes from shared data, not from any single feature being novel on its own.

3. Guidance engine — a recommendation with its evidence attached

	•	Most “find your path” tools are a one-time quiz or a static counselor list.
	•	StudyHold scores a student against their own baseline (not an absolute cutoff), factors in work-style strengths (projects vs. essays vs. exams) and voluntary effort (notes, books, focus sessions), and shows the exact numbers behind every recommendation.
	•	Fully deterministic — no AI API call, no network dependency, no third-party model to expire or drift. Works offline, costs nothing to run.

4. No lock-in

	•	Full export to open JSON and standard iCalendar — a student can leave anytime with everything they entered, in a format anything else can read.
	•	This is a real tradeoff the product accepts, not a marketing line — no retention benefit from locking data into a proprietary format.

5. Honest limits

	•	None of this is market-validated yet — no real-student testing at scale.
	•	The claim is structural difference (ownership model, connected data, transparent recommendations), not proven superiority. That’s still an open question the next step should test directly.
